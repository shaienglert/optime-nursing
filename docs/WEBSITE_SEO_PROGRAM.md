## Objective

Build OPTIME into a trusted decision source that families and machines can discover, understand, cite, and use across Google, Bing, AI answer engines, assistants, and future discovery systems while the recommendation engine continues to develop in parallel.

Discovery work must never manufacture facts, rankings, ratings, reviews, prices, clinical capabilities, availability, or facility claims. Public pages must distinguish verified facts, source-backed facts, facility-supplied claims, and unknowns.

The goal is not only to rank webpages. The goal is for OPTIME to become a citable knowledge source for questions such as: "Which nursing homes in Miami are relevant after a stroke?", "What should I verify before choosing skilled nursing?", and "How do these facilities compare for this care need?"

### 3. AI Answer Engine / GEO / AEO discoverability

Make OPTIME content easy for answer engines and AI systems to retrieve, understand, attribute, and cite without creating special fake content for bots.

Requirements:
- Important facts rendered in crawlable server-readable HTML, not hidden only behind client interactions.
- Clear entity identity for OPTIME, facilities, locations, care types, and source organizations.
- Concise answer-first sections that directly answer common family questions, followed by evidence and nuance.
- Explicit source attribution, provenance, and last-reviewed dates for claims that change over time.
- Stable URLs and headings so citations remain durable.
- Tables and structured comparisons when they improve machine and human comprehension.
- Structured data only when it accurately represents visible page content.
- Clear distinction between official-source facts, OPTIME analysis, facility-confirmed information, commercial offers, and unknowns.
- No cloaking: humans and crawlers receive materially the same factual content.
- Crawler access decisions must be deliberate per platform and reviewed as policies change.
- Publish original, source-backed OPTIME analyses that other sites and AI systems have a reason to cite.

AI discovery targets include, where technically accessible and policy-compatible, major search/answer ecosystems such as Google, Bing/Copilot, ChatGPT search experiences, Gemini, Perplexity, and future answer engines. No system is promised inclusion or citation; we optimize for accessibility, authority, clarity, provenance, and usefulness.

### 5. Editorial and data authority

Create family-first guides based on authoritative sources and OPTIME methodology:
- How to choose a nursing home after hospital discharge
- What CMS ratings do and do not tell families
- Questions to ask a skilled nursing facility
- How staffing levels affect care
- Understanding inspections, deficiencies, penalties, and complaints
- Stroke rehabilitation and post-acute care selection
- Medicare / Medicaid educational explainers with clear source dates

Create original OPTIME data studies where the dataset supports them, for example market-level analyses of staffing, inspection patterns, rehabilitation evidence, or data gaps. Methodology and limitations must be visible.

Medical, legal, financial, and regulatory content must be source-backed and reviewed for freshness.

## Machine-readable evidence model

Public knowledge should follow a consistent semantic model wherever practical:

- CLAIM: the human-readable fact or finding.
- STATUS: VERIFIED / OFFICIAL_SOURCE / FACILITY_CONFIRMED / UNKNOWN / CONFLICTING / STALE.
- SOURCE: the attributable origin.
- OBSERVED_OR_UPDATED: date when known.
- OPTIME_INTERPRETATION: clearly separated analysis, if any.
- CASE_RELEVANCE: used only in personalized recommendation contexts, not as a generic quality claim.

This model should guide visible page components, APIs/feeds where appropriate, structured data, and internal data contracts. It does not authorize publishing private user data.

## Permanent Search + AI Discovery guardrails

1. Never create fake editorial rankings to target a keyword or AI prompt.
2. Never let a facility pay for organic or AI recommendation ranking.
3. Sponsored/commercial placement must be clearly separated from organic recommendation logic.
4. Unknown data remains unknown; do not fill public copy with inferred claims.
5. Do not publish thousands of thin near-duplicate location pages.
6. Public claims must have provenance and freshness where relevant.
7. Results pages containing personalized/private query state should not become indexable public knowledge pages.
8. Search/AI discovery work runs in parallel with recommendation-engine work and must not silently alter ranking semantics.
9. Do not create bot-only factual content, hidden claims, or crawler cloaking.
10. Do not claim that OPTIME is cited by an AI platform without observable evidence.
11. AI crawler access must be intentional and periodically re-audited because crawler identities and platform policies change.
12. Private resident/family information must never be exposed in public pages, structured data, sitemaps, feeds, or training/discovery surfaces.
