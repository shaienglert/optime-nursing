# Resident Placement Success Research Skill

## Purpose
Equip the existing Senior Living Research Agent, Clinical Evidence Agent, Outcome Learning Agent, Resident Needs Intelligence Agent, Family Experience Intelligence Agent, Provider Intelligence Agent, and Chief AI Supervisor to research what predicts successful or unsuccessful adjustment after an older adult or couple moves into assisted living, a nursing home, or a continuing-care community.

## Core Research Question
Which resident, couple, family, facility, staff, social-environment, built-environment, and transition-process factors are associated with or predict successful adjustment after placement, and which factors are supported strongly enough to become OPTIME decision variables?

## Scope
This skill is about Resident/Couple ↔ Senior Living Community placement success. It is NOT caregiver hiring or caregiver–patient matching.

Success outcomes include, when validly measured:
- psychological adjustment to the new residence
- resident-reported quality of life
- resident satisfaction
- family satisfaction
- social connection / loneliness
- sense of home / belonging
- autonomy / perceived control
- safety and avoidable adverse outcomes
- continuity / avoidable relocation or transfer
- successful return to community when the placement is rehabilitation-oriented
- stability of care relationships
- avoidable hospitalization / emergency use when clinically relevant

Do not collapse all outcomes into one score until validation supports doing so.

## Scientific Method
For every candidate factor, capture:
- canonical name
- precise construct definition
- outcome(s) studied
- study design
- setting (assisted living / nursing home / CCRC / residential aged care)
- sample size when reported
- effect size / coefficient / explained variance when reported
- direction of association
- whether evidence is cross-sectional, longitudinal, quasi-experimental, interventional, systematic review, meta-analysis, or qualitative synthesis
- evidence quality
- conflicting evidence
- moderators
- whether measurable before placement
- whether it is RESIDENT, COUPLE, FAMILY, FACILITY, STAFF, TRANSITION, or ENVIRONMENT side
- epistemic class: DESCRIPTIVE / ASSOCIATED / PREDICTIVE / CAUSAL / UNKNOWN

Never upgrade correlation into causality. Never invent an effect size. If no reliable evidence exists, mark UNKNOWN.

## Source Priority
P0:
- systematic reviews / meta-analyses
- high-quality longitudinal studies
- validated resident-quality-of-life studies
- government / regulator studies (CMS, Texas HHSC, AHRQ, NIH, CDC when relevant)
- peer-reviewed long-term-care outcomes research

P1:
- strong cross-sectional multi-facility studies
- mixed-method studies with clear sampling and transparent methods
- validated qualitative meta-syntheses for constructs such as sense of home, autonomy, social connection, and transition experience

P2:
- single-facility or small qualitative studies, used only for hypothesis generation or mechanism understanding

Excluded as evidence for the Canon:
- marketing claims
- provider testimonials
- generic senior-living blogs
- unsupported expert opinion
- star ratings without validated relationship to the outcome in question

## Required Construct Families to Challenge, Not Assume
1. Decision participation / voluntariness of move
2. Preparation and expectation realism before move
3. Self-efficacy / coping resources
4. Baseline mental health and psychological vulnerability
5. Functional status / ADL burden
6. Cognitive impairment / dementia-specific transition needs
7. Social support and family contact
8. Resident–staff relationship quality
9. Resident–resident social fit / opportunities for connection
10. Autonomy, choice, dignity, and control
11. Person-centred care / preference congruence
12. Activities and meaningful engagement fit
13. Food / dining fit where supported
14. Private space, personalization, and sense of home
15. Facility social climate / conflict / cohesion
16. Staffing sufficiency, stability, consistency, skill mix, and leadership
17. Continuity of staff and care
18. Facility size / ownership / structural characteristics
19. Clinical capability fit to resident needs
20. Rehab capability when transition goal is recovery
21. Family–staff communication and relationship
22. Geographic proximity / visitation feasibility
23. Culture, language, religion, and daily-life compatibility only where legitimately tied to wellbeing or care delivery
24. Transition support during the first days/weeks/months
25. Forced/multiple relocations
26. Couple-specific continuity, co-residence, differing care levels, privacy, and preservation of relationship routines

## Couple Rule
A couple is not two independent resident profiles. Research must explicitly distinguish:
- both partners moving together
- one partner entering care while the other remains outside
- partners requiring different care levels
- ability to remain physically proximate as needs change
- preservation of shared routines, privacy, social identity, and mutual support
- risk of separation caused by care-level escalation

Where couple-specific empirical evidence is weak, mark the construct UNKNOWN / RESEARCH-ONLY rather than borrowing single-resident evidence without qualification.

## Evidence States
- KNOWN: directly supported by high-quality evidence or verified source data
- INFERRED: plausible interpretation from multiple supported signals; not user-confirmed and not equivalent to fact
- CONFIRMED: explicitly confirmed by resident/family/provider or validated source
- CONTRADICTED: reliable evidence conflicts with the proposed fact or inference
- UNKNOWN: insufficient reliable information

## Output Gate
A factor may enter Placement Success Canon v1 only if:
1. the construct is clearly defined;
2. the evidence has a traceable source;
3. the outcome relationship is stated precisely;
4. evidence type and limitations are explicit;
5. it is measurable or at least operationalizable;
6. the factor does not encode discriminatory assumptions;
7. uncertainty is preserved.

## Prohibited Behavior
- No numeric probability of placement success before outcome validation.
- No single composite score presented as validated.
- No protected-trait stereotyping.
- No inference of resident diagnosis from browsing behavior.
- No use of age, race, ethnicity, religion, sex, disability, nationality, sexual orientation, or family status as a desirability variable. Legitimate care needs must be represented directly (for example mobility assistance, language needed for safe communication, dietary requirement, or gender-sensitive personal-care request when legally appropriate), not through a protected-trait proxy.

## Required Deliverables
- Resident–Senior Living Success Factors Canon v1
- evidence table with citations and study design
- effect sizes where actually reported
- factor → required-data matrix
- resident / couple / family / facility / transition evidence map
- unresolved research questions
- Version-1 versus Research-Only recommendation

## Learning Loop
Observe → Infer → Confirm → Learn

Outcome learning must remain separate from the research canon. Observed production outcomes may generate hypotheses, but no ranking policy changes until the hypothesis is validated, reviewed, and approved through OPTIME governance.
