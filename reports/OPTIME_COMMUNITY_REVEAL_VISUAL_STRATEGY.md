## Executive Recommendation

Adopt **Direction A: Modern Architectural Photography**, with restrained human details borrowed from Direction B.

The core image should be a real, rights-cleared photograph of a senior living community at human scale: a legible shared entrance, accessible walking route, mature landscape, generous windows, and warm interior light. People may appear as subtle signs of life at later stages, but architecture remains the stable visual anchor.

This direction best expresses OPTIME's promise:

> The more we understand your family, the clearer the right community becomes.

It feels credible rather than simulated, warm without becoming sentimental, and premium without implying luxury as quality. Most importantly, it represents discovery: the community already exists and comes into focus as meaningful needs are understood.

## What Changes Conceptually

### Retire

- Building a house
- Construction stages
- A private-home silhouette
- Drawn walls, roof, windows, garden, and lights
- Segmented progress bars
- Decorative or game-like motion

### Introduce

- A real senior living community as the visual subject
- One session-stable visual story selected from a governed library
- Seven perceptual states of the same image
- One evolving advisor-status sentence
- A continuous image from assessment into the first recommendation
- A verified crossfade from generic discovery imagery to official facility imagery

## Visual Principles

1. **Discovery, not construction.** The image exists from the first question. OPTIME changes how clearly it can be perceived, not what has been built.
2. **Community, not house.** Every exterior must read as shared senior living: a communal entrance, multiple residences or common areas, accessible circulation, and social landscape.
3. **Warmth, not luxury.** Natural materials and good light are welcome. Resort cues, monumental scale, valet entrances, fountains, and conspicuous opulence are not.
4. **Life, not performance.** People should feel incidental and credible. Avoid posed smiles, staged caregiver gestures, birthday scenes, medical uniforms, and tokenized diversity.
5. **Progress, not loading.** The visual state changes only after meaningful decision areas are understood. No spinner, dots, fake analysis, or time-based progress.
6. **Uncertainty remains honest.** The generic image is labeled in accessible copy as a visual representation, never as a candidate facility.
7. **Evidence remains separate.** Image clarity must not imply that more facility information exists, that a community is verified, or that a match score improved.

## Art Direction

### Required subject cues

- Clearly communal senior living scale
- Welcoming, accessible entrance visible in the composition
- Step-free route or gently graded path
- Comfortable exterior or interior shared seating
- Mature landscaping appropriate to the region
- Large windows and natural daylight
- Warm interior illumination available in the source or in a licensed alternate exposure
- Human-scale materials: timber, brick, textured stone, plaster, glass, planting
- Space for responsive crops without losing the entrance

### Exclusions

- Private detached houses
- Villas, mansions, castles, resorts, and luxury towers
- Hospitals, clinical corridors, exam rooms, and medical equipment as hero imagery
- Empty real-estate listing photography
- Ultra-wide distortion or drone views that remove human scale
- Heavy HDR, orange-and-teal grading, artificial lens flare, or obvious generative artifacts
- Visible facility logos in generic assessment imagery
- Images that imply a specific care capability without evidence

### Tonal system

- Early stages: softened detail, reduced saturation, lower exposure, shallow local contrast, cool-neutral haze
- Middle stages: progressively restored edge detail, local color, garden separation, and window depth
- Final stage: natural color, full but not sharpened-to-excess detail, warm windows, subtle entrance emphasis
- Dark areas retain detail; the image must not become a black placeholder
- Skin tones, when present, remain natural at every stage

## Three Mood Boards

The visual companion board is: `reports/optime-community-reveal-design-proposal.html`.

Mood-board imagery is reference material only. Production inclusion requires a completed rights and identity record.

### Direction A - Modern Architectural Photography

**Keywords:** honest architecture, inhabited calm, natural materials, garden threshold, warm windows, accessible arrival.

**Composition:** eye-level or slightly elevated view, 35-50 mm equivalent lens, entrance near the central third, foreground planting or path for depth, no dramatic aerial perspective.

**Palette:** warm limestone, pale timber, muted brick, leaf green, soft sky, restrained brass light.

**Advantages**

- Highest trust because the environment is real
- Strongest connection to the act of finding an existing place
- Regional variation feels natural rather than themed
- Focus, haze, light, and color restoration work convincingly
- Transitions naturally into verified official facility photography

**Disadvantages**

- Requires unusually careful licensing and release management
- Suitable senior-community architecture is harder to source than generic housing
- A single photograph may not contain every desired stage cue
- Weather, crop, and lighting consistency require editorial control

**Mitigation:** commission a small multi-region shoot or license coherent image sets with alternate exposures and generous crop rights.

### Direction B - Premium Lifestyle Editorial

**Keywords:** arrival, belonging, quiet confidence, companionship, ordinary dignity, unposed life.

**Composition:** architecture remains visible, while one or two older adults use a garden, lounge, dining space, or reception threshold naturally. Subjects are part of the scene, not portraits placed over UI.

**Palette:** linen, sage, soft charcoal, daylight white, warm wood, seasonal garden color.

**Advantages**

- Most immediately human and emotionally legible
- Communicates community life beyond the building
- Can represent mobility, companionship, and family presence with dignity
- Strong editorial character when commissioned well

**Disadvantages**

- People can unintentionally imply demographic, clinical, cultural, or financial fit
- Stock imagery often feels staged or manipulative
- Model releases and sensitive-use review are required
- Progressive blur on faces can feel unsettling or dehumanizing

**Mitigation:** never use a face as the reveal target. Reveal environment first; introduce people only as small, fully respectful contextual figures at Stage 6.

### Direction C - Photorealistic Architectural Visualization

**Keywords:** controlled daylight, tactile materials, precise accessibility, cinematic calm, believable occupancy.

**Composition:** a purpose-designed senior community entrance and courtyard rendered at eye level, with subtle interior silhouettes and physically plausible light.

**Palette:** honed stone, warm oak, planting green, soft terracotta, clear glass, amber interior light.

**Advantages**

- Complete control of seven stages, crop, region, accessibility, and lighting
- No accidental facility or brand identity
- Can produce desktop, mobile, dusk, and depth layers from one scene
- Internally created renders provide a clear rights chain

**Disadvantages**

- Can look like property-development marketing
- Small material or human errors quickly undermine trust
- Less direct transition to real facility photography
- High-quality production is expensive and needs specialist review

**Mitigation:** commission an architectural visualization studio, prohibit impossible perfection, and require documentary color grading and accessibility review.

## Final Direction

**Select Direction A.** Use real architectural photography as the primary system. Add restrained lifestyle cues only in Stage 6 and in secondary library images.

Direction A is the only option that simultaneously communicates that the place already exists, supports regional authenticity, and prepares the user for a transition into real official facility media. Direction B is emotionally strong but makes people carry too much semantic weight. Direction C is controllable but risks replacing the current artificiality with a more sophisticated form of artificiality.

## Seven-Stage Reveal

All stages use the same source image and the same crop. No tiles or arbitrary rectangular wipes are allowed. Progress is determined by meaningful completed decision areas, not by raw question count.

| Stage | Perceptual state | Newly legible | Motion treatment | Advisor status example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Orientation | 18-22 px soft focus; 45% lower exposure; 70% desaturation; gentle full-frame haze | Landscape silhouette and overall scale | Initial 350 ms fade from paper tone | Understanding your family's situation... |
| 2. Arrival | 13-16 px focus; entrance receives a broad feathered clarity mask; exposure rises slightly | Shared entrance and accessible threshold | 500 ms focus and light transition | Understanding where care needs to work... |
| 3. Architecture | 9-11 px focus; local contrast restored across facade; color returns to materials | Community architecture, windows, shared scale | 550 ms ease-out; no translation required | Comparing daily support... |
| 4. Outdoors | 6-8 px focus; foreground haze recedes; greens and path detail return | Gardens, walking paths, seating | 550 ms depth separation; optional 2 px parallax maximum | Reviewing rehabilitation needs... |
| 5. Interior warmth | 3-5 px focus; windows brighten; warm interior tones emerge | Reception or lounge depth behind glazing | 600 ms luminance crossfade | Checking verified information... |
| 6. Community life | 1-2 px focus; natural color nearly complete; subtle people or occupied seating becomes legible | A place that feels inhabited and calm | 600 ms local color and depth restoration | Looking for the strongest clinical fit... |
| 7. Ready | Full natural focus and color; soft entrance pool of light; no bloom | Complete community and welcoming front door | 700 ms final settle; entrance glow below 8% luminance change | Preparing your personalized recommendations... |

### Reduced motion

- Apply each stage as an immediate visual state with no interpolation
- Do not use parallax
- Preserve the same differences in blur, luminance, saturation, and local emphasis
- Status sentence changes immediately and remains announced through a polite live region
- The final entrance light is static

### Accessibility semantics

- Alt text at Stages 1-6: "A symbolic senior living community gradually becoming clearer as OPTIME understands your family's needs."
- Alt text at Stage 7: "A warm, welcoming senior living community shown clearly, representing readiness to begin matching."
- Hidden explanatory text states that this is representative imagery, not a recommended facility
- Never place essential text inside the image
- Maintain at least 4.5:1 contrast for status text and controls
- Image click target is at least 44 x 44 px and has a visible keyboard focus state

## AI Status Language

Replace the segmented progress bar and construction labels with one evolving sentence. Only one sentence appears at a time. The sentence describes the current decision area, not an unsupported backend operation.

Approved sentence family:

- Understanding your family's situation...
- Understanding where care needs to work...
- Comparing daily support...
- Reviewing rehabilitation needs...
- Considering location and family access...
- Checking the information that matters most...
- Looking for the strongest clinical fit...
- Preparing your personalized recommendations...

Rules:

- Fade outgoing text for 120 ms, then incoming text for 180 ms
- No spinner, animated ellipsis, typing cursor, or looping pulse
- In reduced motion, replace text immediately
- Do not say "checking verified information" unless the product is actually evaluating governed evidence at that moment
- Never imply that background processing is occurring during ordinary assessment input

## Match Moment

At readiness, Stage 7 completes and the advisor writes exactly:

> We now understand enough about your family's needs to begin finding the communities most likely to fit.

Primary action:

**Find My Best Matches**

The full image is also clickable and invokes the same action. Its accessible name is "Find My Best Matches." The primary button remains visually explicit; image clickability is an enhancement, not the only path.

## Assessment Layout

### Desktop, 1280 px and above

- Maximum content width: 1280 px
- Two columns: document `minmax(0, 1fr)` and visual `clamp(360px, 34vw, 500px)`
- Gap: 56-72 px
- Image aspect ratio: 4:5 at 360-430 px width, or 5:6 where viewport height is constrained
- Visual remains sticky 96 px below the top edge while the active question advances
- Status sentence sits above the image, left aligned
- No card border; image is an editorial surface with a 6 px radius maximum
- A quiet caption below reads "A visual representation of the search, not a recommended community."
- At the match moment, readiness copy and action appear beneath the image without covering it

### Tablet, 768-1279 px

- Single document column up to 760 px
- Image appears after introductory copy and before the active assessment document
- Aspect ratio: 16:9
- Status and readiness action remain attached to the image region

### Mobile, 320-767 px

- Image is always visible above the current question; it is not hidden in `details`
- Full-bleed within page gutters, aspect ratio 16:10, minimum height 190 px
- Entrance remains inside the center 60% crop-safe zone
- Status sentence appears immediately above the image
- No sticky image while typing or choosing; it scrolls naturally to preserve question space
- At readiness, the action spans the content width below the image
- Optional expand control is a familiar diagonal-arrows icon with tooltip and accessible label, not a text pill

## Result Continuity

The generic discovery image remains visible when matching begins. Comparison narration and the first recommendation continue below it in the same document.

Sequence:

1. Stage 7 remains in place.
2. Advisor status becomes "Preparing your personalized recommendations..."
3. Comparison narrative writes below the image.
4. The first recommendation enters below without replacing or collapsing the image.
5. When the first recommendation becomes selected, the generic image crossfades to the facility's rights-cleared official hero image.

### Facility-image handoff

- Duration: 320 ms; allowed range 250-400 ms
- Two images overlap in identical crop geometry; opacity only
- No zoom, slide, wipe, or white flash
- Predecode the official image before starting the crossfade
- Update alt text only after the crossfade completes
- Show a small evidence-safe label after transition: "Official community photography"
- The image transition has no ranking meaning

If the selected facility has no approved image, keep the generic image visually available and display:

> We're currently verifying official community photography.

Do not display a gray block, stock substitute, copied directory image, or unofficial hotlink.

### Hero

- Large editorial image, not a thumbnail

- Provide photo date when known
- Never infer current condition, quality, availability, or care capability from appearance

### Content sequence

1. Why this community appears to fit

4. Community life

## Regional Personalization

Do not encode a state-to-image switch. Use a region profile derived from the known market and select from metadata.

### Region profile dimensions

- Climate: arid, subtropical, coastal, temperate, humid, cold-season
- Landscape: desert planting, palms, live oak, broadleaf, evergreen, xeriscape
- Density: rural/campus, suburban, mid-rise urban, dense urban
- Architecture: stucco, masonry, timber, glass, shaded arcade, deep overhang
- Light: bright-dry, humid-soft, coastal, seasonal
- Care context: assisted living, skilled nursing, rehabilitation, mixed continuum

### Selection order

1. Filter to `APPROVED` assets with valid rights and required crop variants.
2. Prefer exact care-context and regional-profile matches.
3. Relax architecture, then landscape, then climate affinity if needed.
4. Never relax rights, sensitive-use, or quality approval.
5. Select with a session-seeded weighted random choice.
6. Persist the chosen `asset_id` in the assessment draft so refresh, answer edits, and result loading do not change the story.
7. Avoid the most recently seen assets on the same device when at least three eligible alternatives exist.

No state name appears in selection code. New markets are supported by metadata, not branching logic.

## Image Policy and Safety

Allowed:

- Officially licensed images
- Owner-authorized media
- Public-domain assets with verified provenance
- Properly licensed stock
- Internally commissioned photography
- Internally created renders with documented rights

Never allowed:

- Search-result thumbnails
- Review-platform or directory photography without a license
- Social-media images without explicit permission
- Real-estate, news, or map imagery outside provider terms
- Hotlinking unless provider terms expressly permit it
- An official-domain image assumed to be reusable merely because it is public
- A generic image presented as a real facility

Facility-specific media additionally requires canonical identity matching and a documented display right. Identity verification alone does not establish copyright permission.

## Success Criteria

### Brand

- Families describe the experience as calm, credible, warm, and premium
- Fewer than 10% describe it as childish, game-like, artificial, medical, or luxury-focused
- Users understand that the image represents the search, not a specific recommendation

### Interaction

- Users can identify that the image becomes clearer as the advisor learns more
- The visual story remains stable through refresh and result loading
- The match action is keyboard, touch, and screen-reader accessible
- Reduced-motion users receive equivalent progress information

### Media governance

- 100% of displayed assets have a current rights record
- 100% of facility-specific images have identity and rights approval
- Zero generic images appear in facility profiles as substitutes
- Takedown can remove an asset without a deployment

### Performance

- Mobile image payload target below 250 KB per stage-ready source
- Desktop target below 500 KB for the primary source
- Largest Contentful Paint is not regressed beyond the agreed performance budget
- One source image plus CSS/mask metadata is preferred over seven full image downloads
